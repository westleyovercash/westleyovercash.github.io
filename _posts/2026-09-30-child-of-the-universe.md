That day the silence made me look outside. Usually I'd hear an attempt at Bourbon Street jazz marching drunkenly out of their window. 

On the sidewalk below stood a woman, dressed responsibly for Žižkov. She looked from left to right and down at her watch. Next to her stood a child with elbow-length hair pulled over her face and a purple pajama top dotted with a crescent moon and mystic signs of the universe. The child rocked back and forth while clinging to her percussion—a hairbrush in her right hand and a tissue box in her left.

The door opened. A twentysomething guy with a slicked-back fade and tattoos spoke for a few seconds with the woman. They nodded while the woman verified everything with her watch. The child went inside.
