I jumped out of the car and quickly slammed the door. We didn't want any gnats going back with us to Atlanta. 

Kim and I walked across the dirt driveway into the meeting hall in Montezuma. It smelled of cold concrete floors and fried chicken and strange casseroles. I trailed close to Mom and Dad to stop lipstick and old lady smell from getting on me. With each obligatory hug, Dad rewound out loud through the family tree. Aunt Mae was easy to recognize because she looked like Grandma. She lived all the way up in Chattanooga, which made her more of a carpetbagger than us. Or at least that's what some of the corner whisperers said. 

I looked for Jim. I always imagined he would play for the Atlanta Falcons. He and Leah, his sister, came with their parents from Savannah. He would rescue me.
